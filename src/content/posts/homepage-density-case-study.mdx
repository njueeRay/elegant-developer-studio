# Homepage Density Is a Product Decision

The first version of a personal studio homepage often tries to prove too much. It shows writing, work, media, tools, knowledge, lab experiments, status, contact, commands, and personality all at once.

That can look impressive, but it weakens the first promise.

## Density is not proof

A dense homepage can make a young system feel bigger than it is. But the best personal sites do the opposite: they make a small number of strong signals easy to believe.

For Ray Studio, the strongest first-screen signal is not “look at every surface I built.” It is: a design engineer is publishing, shipping, and keeping the system honest.

## The first screen job

The first viewport should answer:

- Who is this?
- What kind of work is this?
- What should I do first?
- Is the site alive?

That is why the revised hero keeps Ray Studio, a positioning line, a real deployment status, and two primary paths: Read and Work. The command menu remains available, but it is no longer the main character.

## Where complexity can live

Complexity still belongs on the site. It belongs in Knowledge, Lab, Uses, projects, related reading, evidence packs, and Command Center.

The homepage should be the door, not the whole building.
